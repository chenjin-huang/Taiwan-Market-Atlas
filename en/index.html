Taiwan Market Atlas, with you in it

This map covers the whole Taiwan economy: the three GDP sectors, about a dozen industry clusters, the major employers, the science parks, the job families they hire for, your skills, and the niches where those skills meet demand. Click any node to see its shortest path to you. The number of hops is how far that part of the market is from you.

Scroll or pinch to zoom · drag to pan · click a node for its path to you

How far each part of the market sits from you

The page computes this from the graph. For each cluster, it takes the average shortest-path length (hops) from your node to every node in that cluster. A short bar means that part of the market can use what you already have. A long bar means you would need intermediate steps first, such as another skill, a different role or a different employer.

What the atlas says

Most of the map is out of reach, and that's normal

Finance, retail, catering, tourism and real estate make up most of the services side's 55.1% of GDP, and on average they sit five or more hops from you. Nothing in them uses sensor physics or GPU kernels. Distance on this map measures how much of your skill set a cluster can use, not how valuable the cluster is.

The large employers are one step removed

TSMC, Foxconn and the memory makers mostly hire process, equipment and test engineers. From your node, they're reached through a job family, not through your skills directly. That's why those roles can feel like a detour. The roles are real, but they weren't built for your skills.

The niches are the shortcut

The ten green niches are the only nodes that link your skills to employers in a single step. Most of them draw on two or three of your skill areas. They sit mostly in Hsinchu and Taipei. Central Taiwan contributes HIWIN, Largan, ELI, NCHC Taichung and the science park.

Taichung is your strongest local cluster

From your Nantun node, the shortest routes lead through the Taichung precision-machinery corridor and the Central Taiwan Science Park, to HIWIN, Largan, Giant, Winbond and AUO. If you'd rather not relocate, start there.

Supply chains connect clusters that seem unrelated

Petrochemicals feed semiconductor materials. Steel feeds machine tools, and machine tools feed robotics. Advanced packaging feeds AI servers. Because of these links, a role in robot vision or AOI still lets you reach semiconductors later.

The thin lines are where independent products start

Hyperspectral food grading (agriculture, 1.4% of GDP) and environmental emission monitoring (EPB) each have exactly one link to the market and one to you. The big employers ignore niches like these. A solo developer with a working instrument can own one.

Bottom line. Of the 200 nodes in this model of Taiwan's market, only the green niches link your three skill clusters to an employer in one step. Your search pool is small, and the employers in it value your skills more than most. That position, a bridge between sensor physics, GPU runtime and evaluation design, is also the starting point for an architect role or an independent one.
